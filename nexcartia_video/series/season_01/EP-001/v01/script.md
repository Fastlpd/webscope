# EP-001 — The Heir Who Was Sent Away
*Full screenplay draft — target 10–12 minutes*

## 1. INT. PALACE — EAST CORRIDOR — NIGHT

Rain threads down tall windows. A distant string quartet rehearses four bars, stops, and begins again. Rex, 22, stands outside a half-open study door, still dressed for a formal dinner. He holds a folded drawing of a dog beside a house.

**FATHER (O.S.)** Tomorrow, the gates will open. He will leave before the household wakes.

**MOTHER (O.S.)** He is our son.

**FATHER (O.S.)** That is precisely why this must be done properly.

A pause.

**MOTHER (O.S.)** You said this would teach him responsibility. You did not say he would be sent away without—

**FATHER (O.S.)** Enough. He has had every comfort. Let him discover what a life costs.

Rex's fingers tighten around the drawing. A floorboard creaks. Silence.

**FATHER (O.S.)** Rex?

Rex looks down the corridor at a family portrait: his parents, younger Rex between them, all smiling.

## 2. INT. PALACE — PRIVATE DINING ROOM — LATER

Three places are set at a long table. Only one plate has been touched. Rex stands. His mother sits with clasped hands; his father remains at the head.

**REX** I heard you.

**FATHER** Then I will not insult you with a softer version.

**REX** How long?

**FATHER** You leave at first light.

**REX** For how long?

Silence answers.

**MOTHER** Your father believes distance will help you understand the world beyond these walls.

**REX** I asked how long.

His mother looks away.

**REX** Is this because I refused to sign the statement?

His father aligns a glass with the table edge.

**FATHER** You have mistaken comfort for consequence. You will learn to stand without the family name.

**REX** I can learn without being abandoned.

**FATHER** You will be provided with enough to begin.

**REX** A room? Money? A driver? You can send all of that. But you cannot send a reason.

**FATHER** There are matters you are too young to understand.

**REX** I'm twenty-two. If I'm old enough to be judged, I'm old enough to hear the truth.

No answer. At the doorway, MILO appears with his lead. Milo is a medium-sized warm-brown dog with a small white chest mark and dark eyes.

Rex crouches, takes the lead and strokes Milo's neck.

**REX (softly)** Come here, boy.

**REX** Can he come with me?

**FATHER** If you insist on taking responsibility for him.

**REX** I do.

Rex looks at his mother.

**REX** I kept waiting for you to tell me this wasn't what you wanted.

She opens her mouth. Nothing comes. Rex leaves with Milo.

## 3. INT. PALACE — SERVICE CORRIDOR — BEFORE DAWN

The palace is awake but pretending not to be. Two guards wait beside a modest travel bag. The older guard looks uncomfortable.

**OLDER GUARD** Your things, sir.

**REX** That's everything?

**OLDER GUARD** What was approved.

The younger guard reaches toward Milo's lead. Rex steps back, calm but firm.

**REX** He stays with me.

They walk through corridors Rex knows by heart. He stops before a childhood portrait.

**REX** He thought this place was forever.

The older guard gives him a sealed envelope.

**OLDER GUARD** Your father asked that you receive this.

**REX** Did he ask you to walk me out?

**OLDER GUARD** No, sir.

**REX** Then why are you here?

**OLDER GUARD** Because somebody should be.

Rex nods. The doors open.

## 4. EXT. PALACE GATES — DAWN

Mist hangs over the road. The palace glows warmly behind Rex; the waking city is cool and grey ahead. A driver puts the bag on the pavement.

**DRIVER** I was told to take you to the address in the envelope.

Rex looks at the sealed envelope.

**REX** No.

**DRIVER** Sir?

**REX** If they wanted to choose where I go, they should have asked me before they chose everything else.

The car leaves. The iron gates close. The latch clicks.

Rex calls his mother. The phone rings until the call ends. He does not call again. Milo nudges his palm. Rex kneels and rests his forehead briefly against Milo.

**REX** I know. I don't know either.

## 5. EXT. ROADSIDE SHELTER — MORNING

A modest covered shelter beside an ordinary waking street. Rex sets down his bag, checks Milo's paws for grit and offers clean water.

He opens the envelope. Inside: a bank card, a short list of rules, and a note.

**FATHER'S NOTE (V.O.)** You will receive no further assistance. Do not use the family name. Do not return until you understand the value of what you were given.

Rex reads it twice. The paper trembles once. He folds it carefully.

**REX** They think the name was the thing keeping me alive.

He looks at Milo.

**REX** Maybe I thought so too.

## 6. EXT. MARKET STREET — LATE MORNING

A delivery rider struggles with a fallen crate. Small household items roll toward a drain. People step around him. Rex sets down his bag and helps gather the items. Milo waits calmly beside a wall.

**DELIVERY RIDER** You don't have to. I've got it.

**REX** I know.

Rex retrieves the last item before it reaches the gutter.

**DELIVERY RIDER** Long morning?

**REX** Long night.

The rider nods toward a modest café down the street.

**DELIVERY RIDER** There's a place near the bus stop that lets you sit with a dog. Not fancy. But they don't mind.

**REX** Thank you.

**DELIVERY RIDER** You helped me. That's enough.

Rex and Milo continue.

## 7. INT. SMALL RENTED ROOM — EVENING

A narrow, clean room: one chair, one table, a window facing a brick wall. Rex lays a folded blanket for Milo, sets down water and a simple dog-safe meal. Milo eats, circles twice and settles.

Rex checks the door lock, then checks it again.

His phone lights up. An unknown number: *You should not have left the palace.*

Rex types: *Did you know?* He deletes it.

He opens his mother's contact and records a voice message.

**REX** Mum... I don't know what you wanted me to learn. I only know you watched me leave.

He stops before sending. Milo lifts his head.

**REX** No. Not tonight.

Rex sits on the floor beside Milo. After a long silence, he takes a small notebook from his bag and writes:

*What do people need when nobody is coming to help?*

It is not a business plan. It is barely a question.

Outside, a car passes slowly. Its headlights cross the wall, pause, then disappear. Through the rain-specked window, a shadowed figure in a dark vehicle watches Rex's room. The figure lifts a phone.

**UNKNOWN FIGURE** He's here.

Rex does not hear. Milo rests his head on Rex's foot. Rex looks toward the window.

CUT TO BLACK.

**END OF EPISODE 001.**

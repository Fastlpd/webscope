## PROMPT 1 — Full episode: “The House of Kings”
Create a polished 8–12 minute cinematic royal family drama episode titled “The House of Kings”, set in the fictional Kingdom of Avelor. Follow the supplied screenplay and shot list in exact story order. Do not invent new plot events, change character relationships, skip scenes, or move Rex's exile into this episode. This is the beginning of the story, while Rex is still living in the palace.

**Story:** Open at dawn on a majestic but believable palace blending pale-stone European court architecture with original African royal design details: carved dark wood, woven textiles, bronze craft, courtyard gardens, navy banners and restrained antique gold. Establish the contrast with a busy, dignified capital city beyond the walls. Inside the palace, 22-year-old heir Rex shares a playful moment with Milo, a very young warm-brown puppy with a small white chest marking. Princess Amara brings Rex evidence that flood-repair work reported as complete is still unfinished. At breakfast, the royal family debate whether the reports can be trusted. Rex and Amara visit the eastern district, listen to residents and see the real consequences of delayed repairs. Amara later checks the archive with Mara Venn and finds a repeated inspection date that falls on a closure day. In a private briefing, adviser Soren Vale urges King Edric to protect the public image of the programme. Adrian is uneasy but has not yet betrayed Rex. Queen Elara challenges the king privately, revealing that she knows more than she has said. End on a majestic night view of palace and city, with the family now divided by a truth none of them fully understands.

**Performance:** Natural, intelligent dialogue; emotionally restrained acting; meaningful pauses; believable family warmth before political tension. Rex is compassionate but not saintly; Amara is precise and active; Adrian uses humour to hide insecurity; Edric is controlled and proud, not a cartoon villain; Elara is gentle but morally conflicted; Soren is polished and quietly strategic.

**Visual continuity:** Use the same face, hairstyle, complexion, age, body proportions and wardrobe for each character in every shot. Rex and Amara should look plausibly related but not identical. Adrian must have a distinct face. Milo must remain a puppy, never suddenly an adult dog. Keep palace geography, furniture, costume details and lighting consistent. If reference-image support exists, attach the same approved character reference to every relevant shot. If a shot changes a face, reject and regenerate it rather than accepting drift.

**Cinematography:** 16:9 master, 24 fps, cinematic naturalism, elegant composition, slow dolly and tracking, measured close-ups, realistic skin and fabric, soft volumetric daylight, candlelit interiors, restrained depth of field. Palette: midnight navy, warm ivory, antique gold, walnut, bronze, muted teal. City palette: warm stone, market colour, lived-in materials and natural daylight. The city must be shown with dignity and variety, never as a poverty spectacle.

**Audio:** Original orchestral score only: restrained strings, piano, subtle hand percussion and warm woodwind. Layer rain-free dawn ambience, courtyard birds, palace room tone, market activity, workshop sounds and distant evening music. Preserve dialogue clarity. No imitation of existing film scores.

**Editing:** Establishing wides, character two-shots, purposeful inserts of report pages and dates, reaction close-ups, motivated transitions. No random montage. No unmotivated slow motion. No generated titles, subtitles, signs, logos, watermarks or legible document text; add typography and readable text in post. End with a quiet cliffhanger, not an action trailer.

**Negative constraints:** No face morphing, no duplicate characters, no extra limbs/fingers, no changing puppy breed/markings, no modern objects inside historical-style palace rooms unless specified, no fantasy magic, no crowns worn casually at breakfast, no melodramatic screaming, no instant resolution, no product advertisement in this episode.

Use the supplied script and storyboard as the source of truth. If the platform cannot generate a long continuous video, render each numbered scene as separate clips, label them by scene number, and assemble them in order. Preserve character references and seed/settings where available.

## PROMPT 2 — Five YouTube Shorts from the episode
Act as a cinematic short-form editor and story producer. From the completed “The House of Kings” episode, create **at least five distinct YouTube Shorts**, each 25–45 seconds, vertical 9:16, with a strong first 1–2 second visual hook, clear emotional progression, and a final line or image that invites viewers to watch the full episode. Do not create five near-identical cuts. Each short must be understandable without the full episode while preserving the series canon.

Create these five cuts:

1. **“A Puppy Has Entered the Royal Family”** — Rex tries to read an official report while puppy Milo playfully steals the page. Start charming and funny; end with Amara's line that Milo may have better instincts than the council. Emotional function: make viewers love Rex and Milo before the family drama deepens.
2. **“The Report Says It's Finished”** — Amara tells Rex the eastern district is still waiting for repairs. Cut between the official report, her notes and Rex's changing expression. End on Rex: “We need to know whether the work was done before we accuse anyone.”
3. **“A Crown Must Look Beyond Its Walls”** — A resident tells Rex that officials came to take pictures but never returned. Rex listens without promising an easy solution. End on: “Judge me by whether someone comes back.” Make this the emotional centrepiece; never frame the residents as helpless props.
4. **“The Date That Shouldn't Exist”** — In the archive, Amara discovers that six inspections share a date when the road was officially closed. Build a quiet mystery, not a loud thriller. End on Mara: “It is an inconsistency. Be careful with the difference.”
5. **“Silence Has Consequences”** — Queen Elara tells King Edric: “Perhaps the council should choose the truth.” He locks away the old memorandum. End with the palace and city in one frame and a short title card added in post: “The House of Kings — Episode 1.”

For every short deliver:
- 9:16 safe framing; keep faces and key action in the central 60% so captions do not cover them.
- Hook in first 2 seconds; captions burned in during editing, not generated by the video model.
- One clear emotional idea; no contextless dialogue.
- Include a 1–2 second end slate with series title and “Watch Episode 1”.
- Export clean master without platform watermark; then upload natively to YouTube Shorts.
- Provide suggested title, 1–2 sentence description, 3–5 relevant hashtags, and a pinned-comment question.
- Do not add fake claims, fake engagement, misleading cliffhangers or unrelated trending audio.

**Quality control:** Verify that each clip is 25–45 seconds, dialogue is intelligible, captions match the spoken words, character faces remain consistent, and no shot contradicts the full episode. If the source episode is not complete, do not pretend these are extracts; create a clearly labelled teaser based only on approved scenes.

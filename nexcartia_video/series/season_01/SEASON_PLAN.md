# Season One — The Heir Who Was Sent Away

**Format:** connected cinematic drama  
**Episode length:** 8–15 minutes  
**Season length:** 10 episodes  
**Story promise:** a tragic beginning and an earned, hopeful ending  
**Status:** development plan; not yet approved for production

## Season arc
Rex loses the protection of his royal identity and is forced into a life where his title cannot buy food, safety, trust, or belonging. His loyal dog becomes his first steady companion. Through small acts of care and a growing awareness of everyday problems, Rex begins building Nexcartia. The princess enters his life after the audience has seen his character and his struggle. Their connection grows slowly, while palace pressure, Rex's hidden identity, and unresolved family betrayal threaten what they are building. The finale offers hope only after the truth is faced and accountability begins.

## Pilot episode — Episode 01 production brief

### Working title
**The Heir Who Was Sent Away**

### Runtime
Target 10–12 minutes; acceptable range 8–15 minutes after screenplay timing.

### Logline
On the night a private betrayal becomes a public sentence, a young royal heir is stripped of the safety he has always known and sent beyond the palace gates. He carries no useful explanation, only the shock of abandonment and the responsibility to keep his loyal dog safe.

### Emotional promise
The audience should feel the loss of home before learning every detail of the conspiracy. Rex is not introduced as a triumphant future founder; he is a young man trying to stay composed while the world he trusted closes behind him.

### Timed beat sheet (draft)
- **00:00–01:15 — Cold open:** Rain on palace glass. Rex hears a private conversation that reveals his parents have decided to send him away. He is not given a chance to explain.
- **01:15–02:45 — The fracture:** A restrained confrontation. His father frames the exile as a lesson; his mother cannot or will not stop it. Rex asks one question that receives no honest answer.
- **02:45–04:00 — The sentence:** Guards escort Rex through a familiar corridor. A small personal item is taken or left behind. The dog refuses to leave his side.
- **04:00–05:30 — Beyond the gates:** The palace doors close. Sound drops away. Rex tries to call for help and realizes the people he expected to protect him have withdrawn that protection.
- **05:30–07:00 — First night:** Rex finds a modest, safe place to pause. The scene focuses on practical decisions, not graphic suffering. He checks the dog's water and settles it before himself.
- **07:00–08:45 — A choice:** A stranger or passerby faces a small problem. Rex helps despite his own difficulty. This establishes character through action, not speech.
- **08:45–10:15 — The unanswered message:** Rex looks at an unsent message to his family, then deletes or saves it without sending. He keeps one object from his former life.
- **10:15–11:15 — Closing image:** In the rain, Rex opens a blank notebook. He has no business plan yet. He writes one simple question about what people need. Cut on the first faint teal reflection.
- **11:15–11:45 — End hook:** A car passes slowly near the street; someone inside recognizes Rex. Their face remains unseen.

### Pilot guardrails
- Do not introduce the princess as a romantic interest in this episode.
- Do not reveal the full reason for the betrayal; plant clues and let the season earn the truth.
- Do not imply Rex already owns Nexcartia or has a working store.
- Do not force a product placement into the exile sequence.
- Milo is Rex's dog. Keep Milo's coat, size, markings, collar, leash, and temperament consistent in every scene and generation.
- Do not turn the royal parents into one-dimensional villains. Show the emotional contradiction between status, fear, guilt, and responsibility.
- End with uncertainty, not a motivational speech.

## Character introductions by season
- **Rex:** pilot; the audience understands his values before his royal identity is fully explained.
- **Rex's parents:** pilot as sources of rupture; their motives unfold gradually.
- **Princess:** episode 03, introduced through her own conflict and curiosity.
- **Sophia, Victoria, Jane:** introduce across episodes 06–07, each with a distinct worldview and independent function.
- **Palace staff / guards:** recurring witnesses to power and its consequences; avoid making every guard cruel.
- **Customers / rivals / collaborators:** introduced only when they create a meaningful business or emotional consequence.

## Episode handoff contract
At the end of every approved episode, update:
- what each principal character knows;
- what each character believes (including false beliefs);
- relationship trust level and unresolved conflict;
- exact timeline and location;
- wardrobe, props, dog continuity, and visual reference IDs;
- current Nexcartia stage and any verified catalogue references;
- promises made to the audience and the next episode's opening question.
